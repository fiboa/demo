… hectares of Dutch farmland have dropped out of the field register since 2009

Every year, Dutch farmers declare their fields to the government for subsidies. Those declarations form the BRP, the national register of agricultural parcels. Stack eighteen editions of it on a 2-metre grid and you can see, for every patch of land, the last year it was farmed. Where that year is before 2026, the land has left the register: built over, turned into nature, or no longer declared.

Where whole areas went

Much of the lost land is scattered: an edge here, a field there. These six areas are different. They are the largest stretches where at least half of every 128 × 128 m cell left the register, the kind of change a new housing estate, business park or nature project leaves behind.

    About the data

    Source
    The BRP (Basisregistratie Gewaspercelen) by RVO, published through PDOK, editions 2009 to 2026, as harmonized in the Fields of the World catalog (CC0).
    The raster
    One Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF of 314 MB in RD New (EPSG:28992) with 2 m pixels. A pixel holds the last edition in which a field covered its centre, as year − 2000; 0 means never. This page reads it with HTTP range requests: only the tiles you look at are downloaded. See its README and AGENTS.md.
    What "gone" means
    Last declared in 2025 or earlier, so not in the 2026 edition. Leaving the register is not the same as being built over: the land may have become nature, a garden or a horse paddock, or may simply no longer be declared. Small shifts of field boundaries between editions also add thin strips along field edges to the total.
    Caveats
    The 2026 edition is still a concept. Loss before 2009 is invisible. Hotspots, municipalities and crops are all counted on the same 2 m grid, so their totals add up to the national figure.
