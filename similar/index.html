Click a field. A satellite model has summed up a year of images of every 10 m of land in 64 numbers, an embedding. This page averages them over the field you click, does the same for every field around it, and lights up the ones that come closest. Nobody told the model about crops, yet the fields it finds alike mostly grow the same one.

    About the data

    Fields
    The newest edition of each collection in the Fields of the World harmonized field data, read from its single PMTiles archive, with the crop each farmer or agency named, in the HCAT crop categories where the collection has them. Field blocks, which several crops can share, are left out.
    Embeddings
    AlphaEarth Foundations Satellite Embeddings, one 64-number summary per 10 m pixel and year (2017–2025), made by Google DeepMind's model from Sentinel-1 and -2, Landsat and more. The page reads a mosaic of them as Zarr straight from Source Cooperative, about 2 MB per square of 2.5 km, and averages the pixels whose centre lies in a field.
    Alike
    The cosine similarity of two fields' average embeddings: 1 is the same, and fields of one area land mostly between 0.4 and 1. The colours start at the middle of the fields compared (the median), so half of them stay dark. Fields are compared within a square of 3 km around the one clicked.
    What it gets wrong
    Ten metres is coarse: a field narrower than a few pixels is mostly its edges, hedges and neighbours. Alike means a similar year as the satellites saw it, not the same crop: winter wheat and barley grow and ripen together, and a field that was flooded, mown late or left fallow parts from its crop. The crops on the map are those of the field data's own year, which can differ from the year of the embeddings.
    Inspired by
    Development Seed's deck.gl-raster embeddings example, which runs similarity search, clusters and a classifier on every pixel of the AEF mosaic on Source Cooperative. This page asks the same question of whole fields.
